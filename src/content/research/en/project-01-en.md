Two atomic dimers can form highly reflective mirrors in a waveguide, creating an atomic cavity. Their high reflectivity also makes cavity–atom interactions difficult to probe through that same waveguide. This project studies a two-waveguide scheme in which an auxiliary waveguide directly drives a probe atom coupled to the atomic cavity.

Using a non-Hermitian effective model, collective-mode decomposition, and Green’s functions, I derive the reflection and transmission response and connect local spectra to the cavity supermodes. The analysis examines how auxiliary-waveguide coupling controls the transition from resolvable Rabi splitting to collectively induced transparency, and how the probe position changes mode visibility and spectral features.

I carried out the analytical derivations, numerical calculations, figure preparation, and initial manuscript drafting. The manuscript is in preparation and has not yet been published.
